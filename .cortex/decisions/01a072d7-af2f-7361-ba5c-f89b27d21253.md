6.4 requires the service to resolve the policy from a `Map<String, ExpiredBatchPolicy>` keyed by the value of the `block_expired_batch` parameter, with no `if`, `switch` or policy enum anywhere in the service. That parameter is declared BOOLEAN and `ParameterConstraints.acceptsFlag` only admits the exact strings "true" and "false", so the bean names ARE those strings: `@Component(BlockExpiredBatchPolicy.BLOCK)` with `BLOCK = "true"`, `@Component(WarnExpiredBatchPolicy.WARN)` with `WARN = "false"`. The constants carry the meaning that the raw value cannot; the lookup stays an identity map with no translation step to hide branching in.

The port lives in `common/extension` (`ExpiredBatchPolicy`, `BatchCandidate`, `BatchCandidates`, `BatchChoice`) and the two implementations in `patterns/strategy`, matching `RoleAccessPolicy`. The port deliberately speaks in candidates, not in `Batch`: `patterns` is not allowed to depend on `modules`, and keeping the vocabulary in common is what lets a policy be written without touching the batch module.

`BatchService.selectFor(productId, quantity)` builds the FEFO-ordered candidates and hands them to the policy whole — the policy, not the service, decides what an expired candidate means. Blocking throws; warning returns the expired candidate with an alert on `BatchChoice`. Flipping one row in `tenant_parameter` changes the answer, which is the acceptance criterion.
