`SchedulingService` injects `List<AppointmentValidator>` and `OverlapValidator`/`RescheduleWindowValidator` need to query appointments — injecting the service back into its own links would be a constructor cycle Spring cannot resolve. The same applies to `ModuleActivationService` and `ModuleDependencyValidator`/`ExistingDataValidator`.

The read side was therefore extracted into two collaborators that hold the repositories and no chain:

- `core/scheduling/AppointmentBook` — `reference(id)`, `record(appointment)`, `firstColliding(proposal)`, `onDay(day)`.
- `configuration/modules/ModuleRegistry` — `catalog()`, `activeModules()`, `definitionOf(code)`, `dependentsOf(code)`.

Both are public in their package (the repositories stay in `internal`), and both services now go through them. `ModuleActivationService` keeps resolving the `ModuleDefinition` before calling the chain, so an unknown module still answers 404 rather than being a chain rule; the two refusal messages were kept word for word so the existing tests still describe the behaviour.

To let the links reason over aggregates instead of rows, `CustomerService`, `PractitionerService` and `CatalogService` each gained a `reference(Long id)` returning the entity. `Practitioner.worksThroughout(TimeWindow)` (with `WeeklySchedule.embraces` / `TimeRange.embraces`) keeps the availability rule inside the aggregate: `AvailabilityValidator` only asks the question.
