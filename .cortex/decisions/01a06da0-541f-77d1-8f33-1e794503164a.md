Consent is never overwritten. `POST /api/customers/{id}/consents` appends a `ConsentRecord` (purpose, granted, source, recordedAt), and `ConsentHistory` — a first-class collection over those records — answers `grants(purpose)` from the *latest* record for that purpose, ordered by `recordedAt` then `id` so two records written in the same microsecond still order deterministically. `Customer.hasValidConsent()` asks for the canonical `DATA_PROCESSING` purpose. A revocation is just a later record with `granted = false`.

`DeactivationReason` is a record whose compact constructor throws `BusinessException` (not `IllegalArgumentException`, which would surface as a 500) when the reason is blank or over 200 characters. Because of that, `DeactivationRequest.reason` deliberately carries **no** `@NotBlank`: bean validation would answer 400, and the requirement is 422 with the business message. Any future request field whose emptiness is a business rule rather than a malformed payload should follow the same shape.

Customers are never deleted — there is no DELETE route on `/api/customers`. `deactivate` sets `INACTIVE` and stores the reason, matching the table's CHECK `(status = 'ACTIVE' OR deactivation_reason IS NOT NULL)`.
