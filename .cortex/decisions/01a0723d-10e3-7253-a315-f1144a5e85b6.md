Task 4.3 requires that publishing a template carrying an odontogram be refused in a clinic without "the corresponding module". No such module existed: the phase 1.1 catalogue holds only dependent, sessionpackage, inventory, batch, insurance, notification and commission, and `template_field.requires_module` has a foreign key to `module(code)`, so an undeclared code cannot even be stored.

`V4__special_component_modules.sql` therefore adds two rows to `module`: `odontogram` and `bodymap`. They are activation flags only — nothing in phase 6 implements them, and nothing needs to. A dental clinic switches on `odontogram`, a physiotherapy clinic switches on `bodymap`, and the same template machinery serves both.

The rule is enforced at two moments, on purpose:

- at publish time, by the check `RecordTemplateService.publish` already performs over `RecordTemplate.requiredModules()`, which reads the `requires_module` the template author declared on the field;
- at render time, by `SpecialComponentFactory`, because every `ComponentFactory` declares the `ModuleCode` it needs. This catches a component whose field forgot to declare `requires_module`, which publish cannot see.

A field that *did* declare its module is filtered out earlier, by `FieldCatalog`, so a clinic that switches the module off simply stops seeing the component in the sheet — no null field, no empty attribute. `SpecialComponentTest` covers both the refusal and the disappearance.

Consequence worth remembering: the module catalogue is now 9 rows, so a test must never assert its exact size. `ModuleActivationServiceTest.catalogReportsEveryModuleAndItsDependency` was changed from `hasSize(7)` to asserting the seven business modules are present.
